# ResearchOS Vision

## What Is ResearchOS?

ResearchOS is an AI-assisted laboratory knowledge system for scientific research groups.

It connects notebooks, experiments, protocols, images, quantitative data, literature, figures, and research assets into one local-first knowledge graph. It is designed to help scientists find, compare, reason over, and prepare research evidence without replacing the lab's official notebook or scientific judgment.

## Why Does It Exist?

Modern laboratories produce knowledge across many disconnected tools:

- OneNote or other notebook systems
- PDFs and literature managers
- GraphPad Prism
- spreadsheets
- microscope image folders
- sequencing pipelines
- PowerPoint figures
- Slack/email notes
- protocol documents
- inventory systems

The problem is not simply storage. The problem is context.

Researchers need to answer questions such as:

- Which experiments used SAG?
- What changed between protocol versions?
- Which GraphPad file belongs to this notebook entry?
- Do our microscopy results match the statistics?
- What does the literature say about this treatment timing?
- Which draft notebook entries still need to be saved to OneNote?
- What should we test next?

ResearchOS exists to make those answers traceable, searchable, and connected.

## What Problem Does It Solve?

ResearchOS solves the fragmentation problem in scientific work.

It helps laboratories:

- keep their official notebook workflow
- avoid losing knowledge in folders and filenames
- connect experiments to files, images, analyses, and papers
- compare experiments and protocols over time
- generate structured notebook drafts from dictation
- ask scientific questions grounded in local evidence
- prepare figures, manuscripts, grants, and review materials from traceable sources

ResearchOS should reduce the cost of remembering, finding, comparing, and synthesizing lab knowledge.

It should not replace scientific judgment.

## Five-Year Vision

In five years, ResearchOS should become a laboratory operating system for research knowledge.

It should:

- connect every experiment, file, paper, image, dataset, analysis, protocol, and figure
- support multiple notebook providers
- organize active experiment sessions
- produce auditable research summaries
- track protocol evolution and performance
- compare lab results with literature
- support microscopy, sequencing, flow cytometry, and quantitative assays
- assist with figure building, methods, results, manuscripts, grants, and patents
- support lab collaboration, PI review, comments, approvals, and audit trails
- run locally, on a lab server, or in a secure cloud deployment
- keep provenance attached to every scientific statement

The long-term goal is a system where a scientist can ask:

> What do we know about this result, where did it come from, how reliable is it, and what should we do next?

and receive a grounded, traceable, evidence-first answer.

## How ResearchOS Is Different

### OneNote

OneNote is a flexible notebook.

ResearchOS is not trying to replace it. ResearchOS treats OneNote as an official notebook provider and adds structure, search, AI-assisted summaries, experiment comparison, asset linking, and knowledge graph connections around it.

### Benchling

Benchling is a full electronic lab notebook and life-sciences platform.

ResearchOS is designed to be modular, local-first, provider-agnostic, and compatible with existing lab workflows. It can work with OneNote, Markdown, folders, files, and future providers without forcing a lab to migrate everything immediately.

### Notion

Notion is a general workspace.

ResearchOS is domain-specific for scientific evidence. It understands experiments, protocols, markers, compounds, images, statistics, sessions, papers, and provenance.

### Traditional ELNs

Traditional ELNs emphasize compliance and structured record keeping.

ResearchOS emphasizes connected research intelligence while preserving the official notebook. It can later integrate audit and approval features, but its differentiator is evidence synthesis across the entire lab knowledge ecosystem.

### Zotero

Zotero manages literature.

ResearchOS connects literature to internal experiments, protocols, data, images, statistics, and hypotheses.

### GraphPad Prism

GraphPad analyzes and plots quantitative data.

ResearchOS connects GraphPad outputs to experiments, notebook entries, images, spreadsheets, statistics interpretation, literature, figures, and manuscripts.

## North Star

ResearchOS should become the place where all laboratory knowledge becomes connected, searchable, explainable, and actionable.

It should help scientists think better, not think for them.
